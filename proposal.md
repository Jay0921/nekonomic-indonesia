# 01. The Opportunity

## Why Indonesia's Pet Industry?

### A Growing Consumer Industry with Recurring Demand

Indonesia's pet-care industry represents a substantial consumer market, with demand supported by pet ownership, changing consumer preferences, and expanding retail access.

According to Euromonitor International's June 2026 report:

# 02. Why Focus on Cats?

## Cats Represent an Important Part of Indonesia's Pet Economy

Industry research indicates that cats represent a large proportion of Indonesia's commercial pet-care demand.

According to research reported by PetfoodIndustry, drawing on USDA Foreign Agricultural Service-related research:

- Indonesia had approximately 5.1 million pet cats in 2023.
- Cat food represented 77.5% of Indonesia's pet food market by value in 2023.
- Indonesian cat food sales grew from approximately IDR 2.1 trillion in 2022 to IDR 2.8 trillion in 2023, representing 32.2% growth.

These figures describe the cat food market, not cat litter sales.

Nevertheless, they demonstrate substantial spending on products purchased specifically for cats.

### Why This Matters

Cats are not simply a minor segment of Indonesia's pet market.

They represent an established consumer category with demand for:

- Food.
- Cat litter.
- Hygiene products.
- Treats.
- Grooming products.
- Toys and accessories.

By starting with cat litter, the proposed business can focus on one product category while retaining the opportunity to expand into complementary pet-care products later.

# 03. Why Cat Litter?

## A Consumable Product with Repeat-Purchase Potential

Cat litter is attractive because it is consumed during ordinary pet ownership.

Unlike accessories such as carriers or litter boxes, litter requires replenishment.

For many indoor-cat owners, litter is a regular household expense.

This creates the possibility of:

- Repeat customer orders.
- Recurring monthly sales.
- More predictable inventory requirements.
- Customer retention.
- Long-term brand relationships.

### Industry Research Supporting Cat Litter

Euromonitor's June 2026 *Pet Products in Indonesia* report identifies cat litter as a dynamic category and expects it to remain among the best-performing categories because of affordable and premium imported products.

This supports the view that imported cat litter deserves investigation as an entry product.

However, the public report summary does not provide a verified standalone Indonesian cat litter market size or cat litter growth percentage.

Do not invent these figures.

### Strategic Interpretation

Cat litter offers an opportunity to develop a business that benefits from retaining customers rather than continually acquiring entirely new buyers.

# 05. How the Business Generates Profit

## The Proposed Business Model

The initial business will operate as a multi-brand pet-care retailer focused on Indonesian consumers.

We will evaluate two or three established or emerging cat litter brands.

Inventory may be sourced through existing Indonesian distributors or directly from international manufacturers, depending on authorization, minimum purchases, and commercial terms.

Initial sales channels:
- Shopee.
- Other suitable Indonesian marketplaces.
- Social media marketing.

The long-term objective is to develop recognized customer demand and eventually expand into authorized import and B2B distribution.

### Three Levels of Profitability

**Gross Profit**

Selling price minus landed product cost.

**Contribution Profit**

Gross profit minus variable costs associated with selling and fulfilling the order.

**Operating Profit**

Contribution profit minus monthly fixed operating expenses.

The website must explain all three clearly.

A profitable product at the gross-margin level is not necessarily a profitable business.

## Illustrative Monthly Sales Scenarios

Assume:

- Contribution profit: IDR 12,000 per bag.

| Bags Sold Monthly | Revenue | Contribution Profit |
|---|---:|---:|
| 500 | 30,000,000 | 6,000,000 |
| 1,000 | 60,000,000 | 12,000,000 |
| 2,000 | 120,000,000 | 24,000,000 |
| 3,000 | 180,000,000 | 36,000,000 |
| 5,000 | 300,000,000 | 60,000,000 |
| 10,000 | 600,000,000 | 120,000,000 |

These amounts are not net profits or forecasts.

Operating expenses must still be deducted.

### Break-Even Illustration

Assume monthly fixed operating expenses of IDR 25 million.

At IDR 12,000 contribution profit per bag:

**Break-even volume = 25,000,000 / 12,000 = approximately 2,084 bags monthly.**

The business would need to achieve this volume, under the stated assumptions, to cover its fixed operating expenses.

### Interactive Profit Simulator

Allow adjustments to:

- Monthly bags sold.
- Selling price.
- Landed cost.
- Marketplace fees.
- Fulfillment cost.
- Marketing expenditure.
- Monthly fixed operating expenses.

Calculate:

Monthly Revenue = Units Sold × Selling Price

Monthly Gross Profit = Units Sold × (Selling Price − Landed Cost)

Monthly Contribution = Units Sold × Contribution Per Unit

Monthly Operating Profit = Monthly Contribution − Fixed Operating Expenses

Break-Even Volume = Fixed Operating Expenses / Contribution Per Unit

Show an explicit warning if contribution per unit is zero or negative, because break-even volume is then not achievable by scaling sales under the same assumptions.

Show results in IDR, with readable abbreviations for thousands, millions, and billions.

# 11. Key Commercial Risks

A credible proposal must identify risks alongside opportunities.

### Competitive Pricing Pressure

Established sellers may already have strong supplier terms, customer reviews, and marketplace rankings.

### Thin Contribution Margins

Marketplace fees, shipping costs, advertising, and promotions may consume a substantial portion of gross profit.

### Bulky Inventory

Cat litter creates storage and delivery costs that may reduce profitability.

### Supplier Dependency

Imported brands may have exclusive distributors or minimum purchase requirements.

### Customer Acquisition Cost

Paid advertising may make a first purchase unprofitable unless repeat customers generate enough additional contribution.

### Working Capital

Inventory purchases require cash before all goods are sold, and unsold stock can tie up capital.

### Import and Compliance Requirements

Direct importing may require additional licensing, product classification review, customs procedures, and appropriate corporate arrangements.

### Risk Mitigation

The initial approach should be conservative:

- Start with a small product range.
- Obtain real supplier quotations.
- Compare local sourcing and direct imports.
- Test marketing with limited budgets.
- Track profitability per order.
- Expand only after validating repeat demand and cash flow.

Our investment thesis rests on four observations.

**1. Established Market**

Indonesia already has a substantial pet-care economy, forecast at IDR 10.438 trillion in 2026.

**2. Significant Cat-Related Spending**

Cats represent a large proportion of Indonesia's pet food demand, indicating an established population of consumers purchasing cat-care products.

Cat litter is a repeat-purchase consumable, and published industry research identifies it as a dynamic product category.

**4. Potential for Scalable Distribution**

A successful initial retail business could provide customer data, commercial credibility, and supplier relationships useful for future distribution expansion.

### The Central Hypothesis

We believe a carefully managed pet-care business can become profitable by sourcing suitable cat litter brands, pricing them competitively, controlling marketplace costs, and retaining repeat customers.

However, the opportunity is not yet financially validated.

The next evidence required is:

- Actual supplier quotations.
- Product landed costs.
- Marketplace transaction economics.
- Realistic customer acquisition costs.
- Verified consumer demand.
- Repeat purchasing behavior.

**The purpose of the initial business is to test these assumptions with limited capital before committing to significant expansion.**
